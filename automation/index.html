What changed

Same business, same number of enquiries, same two people answering the phone.

Before

  • ×The form emailed a shared inbox. It was read when someone had a minute, which on a fitting day meant the evening.
  • ×Median time to the first human contact: 4 hours 12 minutes. Anything after 18:00 waited until morning.
  • ×34% of enquiries got a call the same day. The rest competed with whoever the customer rang next.
  • ×Every detail was retyped by hand into the CRM. Roughly six minutes each, and the typos stayed.
  • ×Follow-ups happened if somebody remembered. Nobody remembered on a Friday.

After

  • ✓The customer gets a reply in four seconds, using their name and naming the surveyor who will ring them.
  • ✓The owner's phone buzzes at the same moment with the number, the ballpark value and a call button.
  • ✓92% of priority leads now get a call within the hour, including the ones that land at nine at night.
  • ✓Nothing is retyped. That alone gave back about 14 hours a month.
  • ✓Follow-ups fire on their own at two days and seven days, and stop the moment someone replies.

What this does not do

It does not sell. It cannot qualify anyone on the phone, it will not rescue a price that is too high, and it has nothing useful to say to a customer who replies with "how much?" and nothing else.

It does not think. The scoring is five rules a human wrote down, not a model guessing at intent — which is why the owner can read the rules and argue with them.

What it buys is the twenty minutes between a customer pressing Send and a human being useful. In a trade where three installers quote the same roof, those twenty minutes are most of the job.

The six questions

These come up in every conversation about automation, so here they are before you have to ask.

1What it is built on

Make.com runs the scenario. The CRM is whichever one you already pay for. A postcode and property lookup fills in what the customer did not type. Telegram carries the alerts because it is the app the owner already has open. One Google Sheet is the safety net. No server, nothing to host, nothing that needs patching.

2Where the keys live

Inside Make's own connection store, entered once, never written into the scenario and never into a spreadsheet. The account is yours and I build inside it, not the other way round. If we stop working together you change one password and keep everything — no hostage situation, no "my agency holds the logins".

3What gets logged

Every run for thirty days: what came in, what each step did with it, what went out and how long it took. That is the table on the owner console. It exists for one reason — so that "nobody called me back" is a question you can answer in ten seconds instead of a row you cannot win.

4Personal data

The data stays inside tools that hold it in the EU or UK. No enrichment service that quietly resells what it learns about your customers. A deletion request is one row removed from the sheet and one search in the CRM, and I write that procedure down for you rather than leaving you to work it out under a deadline.

5How long it takes to build

A scenario this size is six to ten hours. Roughly two of those are the happy path you watched on the first tab; the rest is the failure handling, and the testing that proves it. That second part is the part that gets skipped by people quoting two hours, and it is the only reason the thing still works in March.

6What you get at handover

This diagram, a short screen recording of me breaking it on purpose and it recovering, and a plain-English list of every message it can ever send you and what each one means. Plus the scenario itself, in your account, documented well enough that another person could pick it up.
