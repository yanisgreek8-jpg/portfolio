Templates a team can fill in

A redesign that ends in a PDF nobody can edit comes back in three months. These two studies end in something the client keeps using: a Word report template with real styles, and a set of business emails that work in Outlook with no platform behind them.

Quarterly report — the Word template

Northwind Facilities, an invented forty-site facilities company, sends the same report every quarter. Three people had built it over two years, each starting from the last one’s file. It had to stay in Word, because nobody on their team owns design software.

Six named styles

A heading is applied, not formatted. Nobody picks a font again, and a heading that looks wrong has exactly one cause.

Fields are orange

Every placeholder is visible. Search for the opening bracket before sending and you know none are left.

Fonts that exist everywhere

Georgia and Calibri ship with the file on purpose. A brand font swaps in by editing six styles once.

Contents as a plain list

An automatic table of contents renders empty in every preview outside Word. This one shows correctly in Gmail, Drive and on a phone.

Download the .docx PDF preview

Three business emails, rebuilt as templates

Calder & Roe, an invented four-person advisory firm, sends the same three emails every week and writes each one from scratch. It had to keep working in Outlook and on a phone — no marketing platform, no HTML build, nothing anyone needs training for.

What changed

  • One column 600 px wide, so it holds together on a phone
  • The wordmark set as text, so it can never be stretched again
  • One accent colour, spent only on the button
  • The ask is a real button, 44 px tall, sitting above the signature
  • Signature cut from nine lines to two
  • Three templates instead of one, each with its own job

The first approach email, before and after. The stretched logo is the detail that gives a small firm away fastest, and it is fixed by not using an image at all.
